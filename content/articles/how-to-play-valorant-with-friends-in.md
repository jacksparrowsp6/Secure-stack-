<p><strong>Quick answer: Valorant uses region locks to prevent cross-region play, but you can bypass them by connecting to a VPN server in your friend's region before launching the game.</strong> This makes Riot's servers think you're physically located in their region, allowing you to queue together. The process takes under 2 minutes and works on both PC and console.</p>
<p>If you and your friends are stuck in different regions and can't play together, this guide walks through exactly how region locks work in Valorant, the documented methods to play cross-region, and the specific VPN settings that minimize ping increase.</p>
<h2>Why Valorant Blocks Cross-Region Play</h2>
<p>Unlike games like Fortnite or Call of Duty, Valorant enforces strict region separation for competitive integrity and server performance. Here's how it works:</p>
<h3>Account Region Locking</h3>
<p>When you create a Riot Games account, it's tied to a specific region (NA, EU, APAC, etc.). You can only queue for matches on servers in that region. If your account is EU and your friend's is NA, you literally cannot join the same match lobby.</p>
<h3>Server Proximity Requirements</h3>
<p>Riot's matchmaking system prioritizes players with similar ping to maintain fair competitive play. Allowing cross-region play would create massive ping disparities (20ms vs 200ms), which breaks the competitive experience.</p>
<h3>Anti-Smurf Measures</h3>
<p>Region locks also prevent high-ranked players from creating new accounts in lower-ranked regions to dominate easier matches (smurfing).</p>
<h2>The 3 Methods to Play Cross-Region</h2>
<h3>Method 1: VPN Region Spoofing (Most Reliable)</h3>
<p>This is the most common and effective method. A VPN routes your internet traffic through a server in another location, making Riot's servers think you're connecting from that region.</p>
<p><strong>How to do it:</strong></p>
<ol>
<li>Download and install a VPN on your gaming PC.</li>
<li>Connect to a server in your friend's region (if they're in NA, pick a US East or US West server).</li>
<li>Close Valorant completely if it's running.</li>
<li>Launch Valorant while the VPN is connected.</li>
<li>Riot will detect your new "location" and place you on that region's servers.</li>
<li>Queue with your friend — you're now on the same servers.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Important:</strong> You'll play on their region's servers, so your ping will be higher than usual. Choose the VPN server closest to their physical location to minimize lag.</p>
<h3>Method 2: Create a New Riot Account in Their Region</h3>
<p>If you plan to play cross-region long-term, you can create a separate Riot account registered in your friend's region.</p>
<ol>
<li>Go to the Riot Games account creation page.</li>
<li>Select your friend's region during signup.</li>
<li>Use a different email address than your main account.</li>
<li>Download Valorant and log in with the new account.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Downside:</strong> You start from scratch — no skins, no rank, no progress. Your main account stays in your original region.</p>
<h3>Method 3: Wait for Official Cross-Region Features</h3>
<p>Riot has hinted at potential cross-region play in future updates, but as of 2026, no official timeline exists. This method requires patience and isn't actionable now.</p>

<h2>Minimizing Ping Increase When Using a VPN</h2>
<p>VPNs add some latency because your traffic travels through an extra server. Here's how to keep it minimal:</p>
<h3>1. Choose the Closest Server to Your Friend</h3>
<p>If your friend is in New York, pick a VPN server in New York or nearby (not California). Physical distance is the #1 factor in ping.</p>
<h3>2. Use WireGuard Protocol</h3>
<p>WireGuard adds 5-15ms of latency on average. OpenVPN can add 30-50ms or more. Always choose WireGuard if available.</p>
<h3>3. Connect via Ethernet</h3>
<p>WiFi adds variable latency. A wired Ethernet connection is more stable and typically 5-10ms faster than WiFi.</p>
<h3>4. Close Background Applications</h3>
<p>Streaming services, downloads, and other devices on your network compete for bandwidth. Close everything except Valorant and your VPN.</p>
<h2>FAQ</h2>
<h3>Will Riot ban me for using a VPN?</h3>
<p>No. Riot's Terms of Service don't prohibit VPN use. Many players use VPNs for privacy or to reduce ping to their own region's servers. Cross-region play via VPN is widely practiced without bans.</p>
<h3>Does my friend also need a VPN?</h3>
<p>No. Only one person needs to change regions. If you connect to their region via VPN, you'll be on their servers. They stay on their normal connection.</p>
<h3>Will I lose my skins and rank if I use a VPN?</h3>
<p>No. Your Riot account data (skins, rank, progress) is tied to your account, not your physical location. When you disconnect the VPN, you return to your original region with everything intact.</p>
<h3>Can I play cross-region on console (PlayStation/Xbox)?</h3>
<p>Yes, the same VPN method works. Install the VPN on your console (or router) and connect to your friend's region before launching Valorant.</p>
<h2>Final Verdict</h2>
<p>Valorant's region locks prevent cross-region play by design, but VPN region spoofing is the most reliable workaround. By connecting to a server in your friend's region before launching the game, you can queue together on their servers. While your ping will be higher than playing in your home region, choosing the right VPN and server location keeps it playable.</p>
